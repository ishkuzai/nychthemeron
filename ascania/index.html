Ascania

Das Reich · the largest human power in the world, and the one with nobody in charge of it

Ascania stretches from the Claret Valley on the Vernan border to the Urthan Mountains in the east, and is made up, in the archive’s own words, of a huge number of smaller kingdoms, duchies and protectorates. It is held to be the inheritor of Sarcera, chiefly by itself.

There is no Emperor. There has not been one for fourteen hundred years, and the Ascanians would be the last people on earth to appoint one.

I. The empty throne

The imperial seat at Sarcera stands empty, swept, and maintained at considerable expense. Everybody acknowledges that it is empty. Everybody acknowledges that it ought not to be filled. It is not a political question but a religious one, and the Church has been clear about it for longer than any kingdom now standing has existed.

The Council of Kings meets in that hall, around that chair, and this is the whole reason the Council exists. A council of equals is what a civilisation builds when it has agreed that the chair at the head of the table belongs to somebody who is not coming.

And "Empire" is a translation error. The Ascanians say das Reich, which means nothing more than the realm. Thetch-speakers render it Empire, because they cannot imagine a realm of that size without an emperor at the top of it, and the word has stuck in every chronicle written west of the Urthans. Ascanians find this funny and have stopped correcting it.

II. What there is instead

A great many kingdoms, duchies, principalities, margraviates, free cities and — on paper, and with a straight face — two or three republics, bound by custom, law, marriage, debt and the Church, and by almost nothing else.

Above them sits one office, and it is less than it sounds.

The High King at Ahnenburg

Hochkönig: chosen, not born. He has exactly two powers, and neither of them is sovereignty.

  1. He speaks for the Ascanians at the Council of Kings. When Sarcera asks what Ascania thinks, he answers — and the answer is binding on nobody at home.
  2. He commands the levy on the frontiers. Peace and order on the marches are his: the Wall in the south-west, the Urthan passes in the east, the pirate coast in the north. Inside those marches he commands nothing at all.

He cannot tax a duchy, overturn a free city’s charter, or compel a king of the Low Gothic Kingdoms to do anything whatever. What he can do is arrive, with the frontier levy behind him, and be extremely difficult to ignore. A great deal of Ascanian politics consists of working out how much that is worth in a given year.

"Supposed" is the word his enemies use and his friends avoid. Every generation produces a jurist who demonstrates that the office has no foundation in law, and every generation the office goes on existing, because somebody has to answer when Sarcera asks and somebody has to hold the Wall.

The Seven

He is raised by seven electors at Ahnenburg, upon a stone called the Königsstuhl.

the Kings of Swarden, Midde, Maren, Ermen and Swyndethe Low Gothic Kingdoms
the Duke of Luden, at Baranthe great northern duchy, and the only road east
the Primate, at Ahnenburgthe Church in Ascania

The present High King is Markward of Harland, of the house of Ermenung, raised eleven years ago after a vacancy of three. The kingship of Ermen itself passed long since to a cadet branch of his own house, so one of the Seven is his cousin and votes as he is told — which everybody knows and nobody can prove is improper.

And one of the Seven is not Ascanian. Luden is Wendish — a Slavonic duchy, holding the only road east, standing to the Reich as Pomerania or Bohemia stood to ours: subject in law, foreign in speech, and indispensable. Its towns have two names apiece, the chancery using the second. Its charters are written in the older literary tongue of the east, in Cyrillic, so the chancery at Wendelstatt cannot read the title-deeds of one of its own electors and takes the Duke’s translation on trust when a dispute reaches the Diet. He has never yet been caught supplying a convenient one, which is not the same thing as never having done it. See Ascanian Names §II.

Three fixed places, and a chancery on wheels

The High King travels, because a High King who sits still is one his vassals stop seeing.

  • Ahnenburg, in the Bergland district of Ahnen — the ancestors — where he is raised upon the stone and where he is buried. The regalia stay here.
  • Wendelstatt on the Claret, a free city, where the Diet sits when it sits.
  • Baran, in Luden, where the eastern road begins.

The records travel with him, in something over sixty carts: charters, grants, judgments, the account of four centuries of who owes what to whom. It has never been catalogued and things fall off it. A considerable number of Ascania’s legal problems are boxes that went into a river in a reign nobody now remembers.

III. The shape of it

Two systems of division, which do not agree, and which are both in daily use.

Constitutional — the Low Gothic Kingdoms: Swarden, Midde, Maren, Ermen and Swynde. Linden, now a duchy of Thetland, was a sixth, and is still called one by people being tactless.

Geographic:

The BerglandsHarland, Ahnen, Obana, Ascania, Ludenmark — mountainous and eastern
The Northern Duchieseverything north of the Berglands; in practice, Luden. Wendish-speaking, and Cyrillic-writing
The LowlandsBruckland and Ermen
The HighlandsSwarden, Swynde, Midde, Maren — lower hills running to Verna
Hathingthe far south-west, between the Wilderlands, Ancora and Evander’s Wall. Gundulf Wormred rules from a hall above Kungstol. The Howling Plains lie north, with the monastery of Brunharug

So Ermen is a Low Gothic Kingdom and a Lowland; the other four kingdoms are Highlands; and Bruckland is a Lowland and no kingdom at all. Nobody finds this confusing except foreigners, and the two systems are not going to be reconciled, because each is useful for something different.

Eastwold, under Duke Ansebert Frederick Ramben the Third, is the east of Hathing and not of the Reich — and the name says so, -wold being an old form that the uplands lost. See Ascanian Names.

IV. The name does three jobs

Ascania, the countya small and unimportant district of the Berglands
Ascania, the Reichnamed after it, because the first High Kings came from there
Ascanian, the tongueHigh German; the speech of the court and the chancery

The county is the original. Its counts were raised, the realm took their name, and the house is three hundred years extinct. The district is now worth about as much as a middling abbey and is administered by a steward who is not often sober. Nothing in the Reich is named for the house that presently holds the stone, and nothing ever is, because that is not how the thing works.

V. The Gothic claim

Gothic tribes came out of the eastern highlands into Verna about fourteen hundred years ago, took the imperial heartland, declared themselves Sarcera’s heirs and were fanatical about it. Their empire broke up. Gothic Norland was partitioned so long ago that its pieces are now in Darde, Thetland, Cunderland, Astrey and eastern Lythe, and the Normans of those parts still claim descent from it.

Every noble house in Ascania claims descent from the Goths. The claim founds precedence at the Diet, marriage settlements, and a great deal of litigation.

Here is the difficulty.

Gothic is not the ancestor of Ascanian. It is a different branch — the eastern one — and it died. Ascanian descends from the speech already in the uplands when the Goths arrived. A Gothic charter and an Ascanian one are not two stages of a language; they are cousins, and not close ones.

Families may perfectly well descend from Gothic men, because conquerors marry and their grandchildren speak the local tongue. What cannot be true is what the heraldry asserts — an unbroken descent of blood and speech together, from the Gothic empire to the present peerage.

  • There is a trade in pedigrees. A genealogist who can produce a Gothic descent that will survive a reading at the Diet is a wealthy man. Meister Wendelin Bracht of Wendelstatt is the best of them, and about a third of his work is honest.
  • There are real Gothic charters, in a script and a tongue the court cannot read, in abbey libraries and in the sixty carts. A man who could read them could demolish half the peerage in a fortnight. Osric of Hallowfield can. He is Thetch, he is not rich, and he has been refused entry to three libraries this year.

VI. The tongue, staged

StageWhat it isWhere it is found
Gothicnot an ancestor. A dead eastern cousincharters, a few inscriptions, the liturgy of two abbeys, and a great many forged pedigrees
Old Ascanianthe oldest form of the real languagemonastery documents, glosses, and a handful of charms the Church would rather were not there
Middle Ascanianthe courtly registerthe epics, the love-songs, and the law as it is still pleaded
AscanianHigh, modernthe chancery, the court, the Diet

The law is pleaded in Middle Ascanian, four hundred years old, which advocates learn as a separate tongue. Azade construes a commentary on a dead liturgy; Payasvatī argues in Sanskrit; Ascania pleads in a language nobody has spoken since the epics. Every great power in this setting conducts its most important business in a tongue its own people do not speak.

For the sound-divide that cuts the realm in two, the Low and High forms, the gazetteer and the naming of persons, see Ascanian Names.

VII. Ascania abroad

  • Thetland — kin, rival, and the owner of Linden, which has not been forgiven.
  • Darde — the western neighbour. The Varangar nobility there are Eowan and Nord by descent, not Ascanian, and the distinction matters to everybody but foreigners.
  • The Eastern Kingdoms — one road, from Baran through the Haldoracian Valley to Torse. The Great Empire in the East wants a seat at the Council, and Ascania’s voice is the one that would carry it. The High King can give that voice and cannot deliver what it promises.
  • The Council of Kings — where his precedence is contested annually by Berland, on the grounds that the House of Bering is older than the realm he speaks for.
  • Hathing — holds Evander’s Wall, is counted Ascanian, and does not behave as though it is.

VIII. For play

  • The carts. A box of charters went into the Marbeke two reigns ago. Somebody has found it.
  • Bracht’s ledger. The genealogist keeps a record of which pedigrees he invented. It is worth more than the realm’s treasury and he knows it.
  • Osric. A Thetch scholar who needs a library opened, and a house that would kill to keep it shut.
  • The vacancy. Markward is sixty-one and has no son. The last vacancy ran three years.
  • The levy. The High King’s one real instrument is the frontier levy, and it is raised from men who would rather be at home. Every year it is slower to assemble.

What is still wanting

  • The five Low Gothic Kings have no names, so five of the Seven are blank.
  • The Primate has no name, nor the Church’s standing on the empty throne in any detail — which is the doctrine the whole constitution rests on.
  • Obana is a region with a footnote and nothing else. See the names page for a suggestion.
  • Luden’s Duke has no name, which is awkward in a duchy whose whole interest is that its elector is the realm’s one foreigner and its only translator.
  • No starting region. On the pattern, Ascania’s fourth mood is a frontier — and the obvious one is Hathing and the Wall, not the east.
  • The republics are a joke with no punchline yet: which cities, and on what pretext.
