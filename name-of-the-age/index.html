The name of the age in other tongues

Reference page. English only. How the age-name is rendered in each represented language, and which form belongs where.

The scheme

The three ages take their names from Anachoran, the oldest written human tongue of Vora and the mother of its literature — not from the Imperial tongue, which is younger. A periodisation in Anachoran is therefore the more venerable, and the schools prefer it for exactly that reason.

No age is named for the noon. The height itself is not written.

Three things worth knowing

Four unrelated tongues name the age by the same metaphor, and it is not borrowing. Anachoran dysis, Imperial occasus, Azadean ghorub and Ascanian Untergang all mean the setting and the west — or the setting and the ruin — without any one having taken the word from another. The quarter where the sun goes down is the quarter where things end, and every people works this out for itself.

Azadean has one word for the hour and the age alike. 'Asr (عصر) means both the late afternoon and an epoch, with no felt distinction between the senses. An Azadean saying this is the 'asr may be speaking of the time of day, of the present century, or of the condition of the world, and the ambiguity is not regarded as a defect.

Only the Ascanian word carries a verdict. Untergang is a ruin before it is a sunset. The Ecclesia has twice attempted to discourage its use in sermons and has twice failed, the clergy of the Berglands being attached to it.

Cautions

  • Vedan. Sandhyā is the technical term for the twilight between two ages rather than an age itself — right or subtly wrong according to whether the present age is a thing in itself or merely the gap before the next. Provisional.
  • Thetch. The Setting reads plainly and is what a farmer would say. Dysis is what his priest would say, and both are correct.
  • Superseded. This page formerly gave Vespera as the name of the third age, on a Latin scheme (Aurora / Occasus / Vespera). That is replaced by the Anachoran scheme above. Occasus survives as the Imperial learned form.
