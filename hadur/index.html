Hadur

Goatmen · the Cerani, as the Old Empire had it Children of the Wilds

It is said that they were originally men from a southern kingdom.

The Hadur are the youngest of the six peoples and the only one that was made. A servant of the Demon Lord Marach warped and twisted the bodies of men out of a southern kingdom and forced them into his master’s service during the War of the Waning Moon. The servant was never defeated; its power merely waned with time, and as it waned some of the goatmen — perhaps feeling their lost humanity resurface — began to question an obedience that had never before been questioned. They fled. Their founding leader guided them across the Great Desert, and those who survived the crossing made what life they could upon the southern savannah.

Everything about the Hadur follows from that paragraph. They are not a wild people. They are an injured one, and their place among the Children of the Wilds is a matter of hospitality rather than of nature.

Body and mind

Men describe them as something between man and goat, which is accurate and tells one nothing. They bear great horns and cloven hooves, long cow-like tails, and are covered head to foot in shaggy wiry hair from black to light brown; the males grow long beards from the chin. Despite the resemblance to goats they are omnivorous, and a great many of them prefer meat.

They are widespread throughout the Old World and have traditionally been nomadic. Their origins, as every document in the archive is careful to say, are steeped in mystery — which is to say that the account above is known to scholars and not to the Hadur, who have their own.

Child of the Moon. The Hadur embody the essence of their mother goddess, the primal wanderer, and wax and wane with her: furious beneath her full form, and vile in her absence. This is the defining mechanic of the race and should be built out as a cycle rather than a bonus — a people whose capacity varies with the calendar, and who plan their wars by it.

Conquest. Named in the source and left blank.

Base attributes. Not recorded.

Faith and magic

The Hadur are shamans. Many of them use shamanic magic in open battle, calling upon spirits and upon eldritch deities for aid — a practice that horrifies the Ecclesia twice over, once for the spirits and once for the deities. Their mother goddess is the moon, the primal wanderer, and their religion is therefore a religion of motion: nothing is owned, nothing is settled, and the holy thing is the thing that passes overhead and does not stop.

This is the deepest reason for the quarrel with the Children of the Heavens, deeper than any grievance over land. A faith whose central image is the wanderer cannot be reconciled with a faith whose central work is the ordering and taming of Creation. One of them must be wrong about what the world is for.

The clans

The Hadur have no state and want none. They are organised in clans, which follow their herds and their leaders and take service where service is worth taking.

In the south, on the savannah beyond the Great Desert, is the oldest settlement of them — such as it is — and the memory of the crossing.

In the north, upon the steppe beyond the Eastern Kingdoms' borderlands, ride the three clans that have taken Svelekebá’s pay against the Great Settlement. They bring horse, shamans and speed, and they are the reason the Empire’s surveying parties cannot work beyond the range of a stockade.

Everywhere else, in ones and twos: Hadur appear throughout the Old World as mercenaries, drovers, guides and outlaws, in places where no clan has been seen for a generation.

In the war

The Hadur are the war’s cavalry and its deniability. They are not subjects of Svelekebá, and so Svelekebá’s Emperor may truthfully say he has not sent them; they are not an army, and so the Empire may truthfully call them bandits. Both statements are lies in substance and true in form, which suits everybody except the people living on the frontier.

Their grievance against the Great Settlement is specific and old: they have been enslaved once already, and they regard a surveyed line as the first stage of the process. A Hadur does not look at a stockade and see a town.

Playing one

The outrider, the shaman, the character with no home to return to. The Hadur is the only playable race whose origin is a documented atrocity, and the question the character carries is whether that is an excuse, an explanation, or neither.

Child of the Moon is a gift to a gaming table: a party that includes a Hadur acquires a calendar, and a reason to attack on one night and hide on another.

What is still wanting

  • No Hadur has a name. Not one, anywhere in the archive — no clan, no chief, no founding leader, no mother goddess by her own name. This is the single largest gap of the six races and the one most worth filling first.
  • Conquest is an empty trait.
  • No attributes.
  • The southern kingdom they were taken from is unnamed, as is the servant of Marach who took them. Both would repay a page.
  • The Ecclesia’s doctrine on the Hadur is undrawn. If what was made cannot have a soul, then a people made out of men by a demon is a theological problem rather than a military one, and every missionary on the frontier is standing on it.
