Hyn'ral

Dwarves · the Stoneborn Children of the Heavens

Hyn’ral is an old transliteration of a dwarven endonym, perhaps related to ûnar, a singulare tantum meaning simply person. Dwarf comes out of Gothic folklore and may be taken as derogatory, though few of them trouble to say so.

The Hyn’ral are the keepers of the record. They are not the oldest people of the Old World, nor the most numerous, nor the most powerful; they are merely the only ones who wrote everything down and kept it, and so the history of every other nation is, at bottom, a history the dwarves compiled.

Body and mind

Short, broad and heavily built, with little difference between the sexes: both are stocky, both grow thick body hair, and both wear beards. The exception is in those communities which have lived among men for several generations, where the women commonly shave to meet human notions of beauty. A dwarf in comfortable circumstances may live two hundred years, twice a man’s span and more. Some grow lethargic in winter, to the point that it may be called a half-hibernation.

Their intelligence exceeds a man’s on average, and is spent almost entirely upon craft, engineering and architecture; they make poor sorcerers, and the reason is disputed even among themselves.

Tough Hide. Resilience increased by a twentieth.

Thick Skull. Whether from want of magical faculty or from a settled habit of grounded, logical thought, a Hyn’ral finds it difficult to accept an uncomfortable truth. It is often a hindrance. It is also why a dwarf can look at a thing out of the Astral and come away merely annoyed: losses of sanity are reduced by a quarter.

Base attributes. Strength 12 · Stamina 11 · Resilience 13 · Agility 7 · Wisdom 11 · Intellect 12 · Spirit 11 · Perception 9 · Charisma 11.

The chronicles

Dwarves reckon time in Ages, each of some thousands of years, divided by the founding or the fall of great nations. Since the Second Age they have kept the Adranal, the Chronicles — more exactly, Histories. A single Adran treats of one place, person, object or idea. The Adran em Talrad, the Dwarven Chronicle, runs from Adranogh, the Praehistoria, to the Adran em Sarak Hiran, the Chronicle of the Third Age; the current Fourth Age is not included, being still in progress. Above them all stands the Adran em Vorabagh, the history of the entire world, of which the dwarven chronicle is only a section.

It is for this that the Hyn’ral are held the most reliable source of history in the Old World, and it is why a party that needs to know what truly happened must eventually go and ask a dwarf.

Their own account of their origin is that they were made by giants to mine the deeps in the Age of Gods, and that the gods, having defeated the giants, took pity and spared them. That they so closely resemble men, and may perhaps breed with them, suggests a common origin instead. The dwarves have read that argument and are not persuaded.

The nations

Haldorak. A mountain range in the north, and the city built within it: the greatest dwarven city that ever stood, its halls wide enough for giants to walk in, its kings famous for eccentricity and obscene wealth. The sixth King of Haldorak had himself turned into a bronze statue in old age by a ritual whose method is lost. Haldorak ruled, or at least overshadowed, Voralan for most of the Third Age — five thousand seven hundred and fifty-three years — and then shut its doors and has not been heard from in over two thousand years. The Silence of Haldorak is reckoned by every other dwarven nation as the beginning of the Fourth Age. The favoured explanation is that it withdrew after the fall of the Old Empire and some years of bad diplomacy with the newly independent human kingdoms, which explains nothing about why no word was ever sent to its own outposts.

Haltelan. Allied with Berland; the Stanfell range is named for its capital, Stanburg, and the highest peak for it also. Ruled by Gartelan Drakmal, last of the Gartelan — Stonebeard — dynasty. It began as a distant outpost of Haldorak and became a kingdom by default when the Great City fell silent.

Haluran. The Border-City, Settlement-at-the-Edge, in the mountains between eastern Sarceria and western Lesser Firthland. Built to bind Haldorak to Sarcera, it is now the centre of dwarven commerce for its central position.

The Haldan. The old outposts scattered through Voralan, militarised in origin and grown into towns and cities, linked to Haldorak by caravans that came every few months until they stopped.

The hill dwarves. A rural sub-people of the villages about the Haldorak Mountains, only distinguishable from their mountain cousins since the Silence. They are a recent phenomenon and a slightly embarrassing one.

Obylen. Not a dwarven state but an imperial province in the Urthan mountains, administered from Kolotz of the hot springs, with its military centre at Gorenburg. It lives by mining and by its links to the mountain dwarves, and it is where dwarven interests and the Eastern Kingdoms' war meet.

A hidden dwarven kingdom is rumoured somewhere in the mountains of the Westerlands. Nothing is known of it that is not rumour.

In the war

The Hyn’ral have not joined it and do not wish to. Their difficulty is Obylen: the Empire’s mines are dwarf-worked, the Great Settlement needs iron, and iron comes out of the ground the dwarves are standing on. One raid that kills dwarven miners rather than imperial soldiers brings Haltelan to the table, and Haltelan brings Berland, and Berland brings the Council. The whole northern war can be enlarged by a single misdirected arrow, and both sides know it.

Playing one

The dwarf is the party’s memory and its engineer, and the character least likely to be broken by what the campaign turns up. Thick Skull is a double-edged trait and should be played as such: the same stubbornness that keeps a dwarf sane in the Astral keeps him arguing with the evidence long after everyone else has accepted it.

The standing dwarven plot is Haldorak. Two thousand years of silence, a sealed door, an unexplained abandonment of their own people, and an entire nation’s worth of chronicles behind it that nobody has read.

What is still wanting

  • The Dwarven Ages are not anchored to the general chronology. The Third Age’s five thousand seven hundred and fifty-three years are precise and float free.
  • The hill dwarves have no section, only a heading, in the source document.
  • Whether Haldan Khur and Haldan Urzon are among the Haldan outposts or separate kingdoms is not settled; they appear in the Lore Overview as kingdoms and in the dwarven document not at all.
