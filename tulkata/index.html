Tulkata

Trolls · ogres · orcs Children of the Wilds

An old race. They have inhabited the world since long forgotten aeons, and live as one amidst the wilds.

The Tulkata are the most widespread people in the world after men, and the most varied: they are found through the Old World, the Westerlands and the islands of the seas, and they come in all shapes and sizes accordingly. They are also the only people of the Children of the Wilds who keep an empire, and the chief military power opposing the Children of the Heavens.

Troll, ogre and orc are all human words, applied at various dates and with various degrees of contempt. The archive uses them in quotation marks, and so should anybody writing in the setting’s own voice.

Body and mind

Broad-shouldered, barrel-chested, thick-limbed, with wide strong jaws that give most of them a markedly masculine cast whatever their sex. The skin runs in shades of red according to where they live; the hair takes almost any colour, and the northern groups carry far more of it on the body than their southern cousins, which gives the two populations a noticeably different look. Some have a pair of short tusks rising from the lower jaw. The eyes are often described as crazed, which says as much about the describer as the described.

Their physiology is largely that of a man, with one marked exception: the liver is much enlarged, to deal with a diet heavy in meat. They tolerate raw flesh, spoiled food and bad water with little trouble, and their resistance to disease and to poisonous foods is proverbial.

Adaptability. The Tulkata are not the most widespread race for nothing. Environmental effects are reduced by a twentieth at once, and adapted to altogether if endured longer than a month. A Tulkata army that survives its first winter in a new country is thereafter a native of it.

Devour. A Tulkata may eat a nearby corpse, reducing hunger and restoring energy. It is worth stating plainly that this is as much a logistical fact as a horror: a Tulkata force does not need the supply train that an imperial one does, which is the whole reason the frontier war cannot be won by cutting roads.

Base attributes. Not recorded. See What is still wanting.

The nations

The Tulkata Empire lies east of Voralan, through the forests of Tucania and beneath the mountains that mark the easternmost limit of human knowledge. Many of the tribes of the region are loyal to it. Some are not, and the distinction is the hinge of the northern war.

Svelekebá, the bear-wood, is the Tulkata kingdom whose forests and lakes the Eastern Kingdoms' surveyors have begun to mark out under the Great Settlement. It is one realm of the Empire and not the whole of it, and it is fighting with three Hadur clans in its pay and no declaration from its Emperor. (The name is provisional — see below.)

The southern trolls are another world entirely. The Fakal dominate Gantumba, which the Kothari call Janubya, together with the Thandi, and between them they cut the Ganda people off from any contact with the north. They keep a caste system of priests, warriors and peasants; their priests live into their sixties and seventies while the lower castes die young of violence. They are ruled by the P’acha ti Kuen ti Mabu, King of the Nurudi, from Nurud’al, styled Fadu ti Fada, King of Kings, and Fadu ti Kal Mas, King of All Lands. Their high priest at Nurud’al is Vem E’cha. The five dialects of their speech are Nurudi, Fakali, Mutu, Thandi and Island — or, by another account, Ganda in place of Island.

It could fairly be argued that the southern trolls descend from a classical civilisation of the ancient era. Nobody in the north has read a word of their literature.

The Eborian and Voraic trolls are gone. They existed in prehistory and do not now.

The islands of the Outer Sea, south-east of the Old World, hold troll cultures and civilisations of their own.

Faith

The southern trolls worship the adwa — powerful animistic spirits, from Proto-Kajua patoka, by fatók and haduo, to adwa. One who dedicates himself to a particular adwa is an ugoti. Jah, Lord, serves as an epithet for several of them and, in some mouths, for one in particular. Auk Bela is the patron god of the Nurudi. The common blessing is Ei p’acha ti Jah sen’ada — glory of Jah be upon you.

The northern Tulkata are less well recorded. What is clear is that the Church recognises no distinction between the adwa and anything else the Wilds venerate, and has condemned the whole of it together.

History they remember

The Second Great Conquest, launched about a century after a devastating defeat, under the Fakali King of Kings Jim’adwa: the south-east of the Old World fell swiftly and the kingdoms of Nuruda and Mutu were founded. A Thandi general, Swada’zanda, led the Buan’zu Incursion north through the easternmost Old World and reached the far-eastern mountains that divide Tucania from the elven lands beyond. Jim’adwa died at the Battle of Khammad, on the Khammad Plain, in Qafalar.

The mountain kingdoms of Tsevrokh and Khargara still tell of a war with demons in forgotten aeons. It is not impossible that they are telling of the same march.

In the war

The Tulkata are the Heavens' only peer in the field. They do not need roads, they do not need supply, they adapt to the country within a season, and they are as numerous as men. What they lack is a decision: the Emperor has not committed, Svelekebá is fighting a war the Empire has not declared, and the loyalty of the surrounding tribes is uncertain. A Tulkata campaign is therefore political before it is military, and the question a Tulkata party will face is not how to beat the Empire but how to make their own Emperor move.

Playing one

The Tulkata is the Wilds' soldier, frontiersman and quartermaster — the character who can live where the party cannot and knows what is safe to eat. Adaptability makes a Tulkata the natural guide for any expedition into hostile country; Devour makes him the character the rest of the table has to decide how to feel about.

What is still wanting

  • No attributes, and only one trait with a mechanical effect. Of the three Children of the Wilds, the Tulkata are the least finished in system terms, and they are the faction’s main strength. This is the first gap to close.
  • The Emperor has no name, no seat and no title, where the southern King of Kings has a full court, a high priest, a patron god and a formula of blessing.
  • Svelekebá is a working name, compounded from the attested Uzda svele, bear, and kebá, forest. The kingdom is needed; the name is the author’s to settle.
  • The two troll language families are unplaced. Uzda — with its descendants Uzda, Vari and Aghun — and Cruntish, with its five dialects, are both filed under troll speech and nowhere related to one another. These pages assume Uzda in the north and east and Cruntish in the south.
  • The contradiction over range. Introduction to the Project makes the Tulkata widespread throughout the Old World; the Lore Overview says the Fakal are the only trolls left in it.
