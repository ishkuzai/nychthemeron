Kar'aqa

The Ravenmen Children of the Wilds

Or, some would argue, they have not much sanity to lose in the first place.

An ancient race, native to the primordial forests, and the strangest of the six. The Kar’aqa possess as much knowledge of cosmic and astral lore as the Do’ari — and most of theirs covers matters far darker than anything the Do’ari’s ancestors ever thought it wise to study. They are not the Wilds' soldiers and never have been. They are its mind, and the other two peoples of their own faction are a little afraid of them.

Body and mind

The archive is sparing about their bodies and generous about their minds, which is itself informative: everyone who has written about the Kar’aqa has been more interested in what they know than in what they look like.

They do not possess the Do’ari’s innate talent for sorcery. What they possess instead is a tolerance: they can understand cosmic truths without losing their minds, and through that tolerance they achieve far greater feats than a more gifted people dare attempt. A Do’ari sorcerer must stop before the thing that would break him. A Kar’aqa does not have to stop.

Eldritch Mysticism. A long history of experimenting with foul sorcery and witchcraft, and a strong tolerance of mind-warping effects: losses of sanity are reduced by half. Only the most horrific things leave any lasting mark upon a Kar’aqa at all.

Base attributes. Not recorded.

Rakatu and the Highlands

Their largest city and their capital is Rakatu, set among the mountain forests of the far east, in a region called the Arkashir Highlands — beyond the Tucanian wilderness, beyond the easternmost reach of the Tulkata Empire, at or past the edge of what men have mapped. It is nearer to the elven lands of the furthest east than to anything in Voralan.

This remoteness is the chief fact about Kar’aqa politics. They are a power of the Children of the Wilds who are not within reach of the Children of the Heavens and cannot easily be made to care.

Speech

Kar’aqa languages are highly tonal, with a small inventory of consonants and vowels and a very large one of tones: flat at three levels, rising, falling, peaking and dipping. They have clicks — dental, palatal and lateral — which no other people of the Old World uses, and a uvular stop. Meaning is carried by pitch to a degree that makes the tongue nearly unlearnable by men, and unwritable in the Abecedarium Imperii.

Faith

Not recorded, and perhaps not applicable. What the Kar’aqa do is not obviously worship. They read, and they experiment, and the archive calls the results foul sorcery and witchcraft — though the archive is written in the Common Tongue, by men, for the use of a Church that has already made up its mind.

The honest formulation is this: the Kar’aqa know more about the shape of the cosmos than any other mortal people, including what the Church teaches about it, and they have never once offered an opinion on the subject to anybody who asked.

In the war

They have sent observers. That is all, and it is a great deal, because nobody sends an observer to a war he means to stay out of.

The Kar’aqa interest in the northern frontier is not territorial — Tucania is a thousand miles from the Arkashir Highlands and worth nothing to them. It is that the Great Settlement will open ground that has not been opened in recorded time, with surveyors and miners and engineers, and the Kar’aqa have a shrewd idea of what is under some of it.

The likeliest first meeting of the two powers' scholarships — Do’ari and Kar’aqa, equal in depth and opposite in temper — will happen on that frontier, in a tent, with armies a day’s march away. It is the single best scene the setting currently has going spare.

Playing one

The occultist who does not flinch. Where the Do’ari character’s limit is sanity, the Kar’aqa’s is other people: a party will let a Kar’aqa read the thing nobody else can read, and will then have to live with a companion who has read it and is unmoved.

The Kar’aqa is also the natural outsider’s outsider — a Children of the Wilds character with no stake in the Wilds' war, who may be playing an entirely different game from everyone else at the table.

What is still wanting

  • No description of the body, beyond the name Ravenmen. Whether that is a matter of feathers, of beaks, of ravens kept, or of nothing more than a human nickname is not stated anywhere.
  • No attributes, and one trait.
  • No names. Not a chief, not a scholar, not a street in Rakatu.
  • The Arkashir Highlands are unplaced on any map beyond "the far east".
  • Their relation to the ravens of the bestiary — which are given Tool Use and Mimicry as traits, and are the only beast in the list so distinguished — is unexamined and looks deliberate.
