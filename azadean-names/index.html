Azadean Names

An onomasticon for Azade — three ages of the tongue, three hands, and about a hundred names

Names marked † are already in the archive and are fixed. Every name is given in the hand of its own age: Old Azadean cut in cuneiform, Middle Azadean in Pahlavi, and the living tongue in the Perso-Arabic letter. The romanisation follows only so that one stage may be seen descending from the next.

The ideograms, and what became of them

Old Azadean did not spell its greatest words. It had a single sign for each, cut whole:

Middle Azadean took this habit and made a system of it. The Pahlavi scribe writes the Aramaic word and reads the Azadean one: he sets down 𐭬𐭫𐭪𐭠 — m-l-k-ʾ, which is Aramaic for king — and says šāh. Several hundred words are written this way. A man may therefore read a Pahlavi inscription aloud without once uttering a sound that is written in it.

This is the same disease as the Zend, and in the same civilisation: the gloss has eaten the text, and the writing has eaten the speech. An Ardavan priest construes a commentary on a tongue nobody speaks, written in letters that spell a different language again. He is three removes from what he is saying and does not find this strange. Nobody has ever found it strange. It is simply what learning is in Azade.

II. What the classical names turn out to be

Three regions carry a classical name beside the native one — Engonia †, Asania † and Caphasia † — and two towns of Bagad, Narcola † and Marscalat †, are not Azadean names at all.

The usual assumption is that these are foreign corruptions. They are not. They are older than the Azadean names beside them, because the Anachorans wrote the country down when they first met it and have not changed their spelling since, while the Azadeans have gone on speaking. Daracia preserves Old Azadean drayahyā; the living word is daryāgān.

Which makes Engonia the joke of the realm. The nativist dynasty renamed the central region to purge a foreign word from its own country. The word was not foreign. Engonia is the Anachoran spelling of Old Azadean hangāna, the gathering-place — the oldest name any part of Azade possesses. They erased their own antiquity, in the belief that they were defending it, and the only people who know are the Berdakani, who read both alphabets.

Four rules

  1. -gird is royal. Only a king founds one, and the name commemorates him or his glory, never a feature of the ground. It is the one element that carries a claim.
  2. -ābād is agricultural: somebody put water on the land, which in Azade is the most honourable thing a man can do to a landscape.
  3. Classical names only in Bagad and on the Daracian coast, thinning eastward to nothing. The proportion is a map of how deep Sarcera went.
  4. Let Kafestan refuse the system. Its hill-names should not parse: Ghorm, Kafs, Tolka, Urzam, Hazba.

The one worth keeping. Trisindhu and Trihind are the same name: the sound that is स in the Vedan tongue is ه in the Azadean, regularly and across the whole vocabulary. It is not a borrowing but the signature of the family both belong to, lying in plain sight in the name of a country. A scholar in either place could see it in an afternoon. None has, because the men who read Azadean and the men who read Vedan do not read each other, and the one people placed to notice are the Berdakani, who have not thought it interesting.

What is still wanting

  • Farragird is a proposal, and the capital of the central region is the one name the archive conspicuously lacks. If it is to be something else, it should still be a -gird.
  • No name for the founder of the faith, and the whole theology hangs off him.
  • Vidarne’s dynasty has no name — the houses of Asania have twelve and the ruling line none.
  • The heterogram list is one word long. A proper Azadean scribe would have some hundreds, and a page of them would be the most characterful thing in the setting’s whole apparatus.
