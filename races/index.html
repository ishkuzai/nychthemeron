The Peoples of the Old World

Six peoples carry the setting, three to each of the two powers. Everything else that walks and talks is a neighbour, a nuisance or a monster, and is listed further down.

A warning carried over from the setting’s own documents. Elf, troll, dwarf, demon and goatman are not to be read in their usual senses. They are what men reached for when they had no other word, and most of them are mildly insulting. Where a people has an endonym, this wiki uses it.

Everyone else

Neutral by habit. Many people of every race stand outside the quarrel, for trade, for adventure, or from conviction.

Gnomes — the smallest mortal people, seventy to eighty centimetres, with otter-like tails and a mane of thick fur. Subterranean, burrowing in hills and under great trees, and far better housed inside than the outside of a burrow suggests. Short-lived by reputation and perhaps not in fact. Universally regarded as pests and thieves, and called goblins, kobolds and hobs accordingly — all of them insults. They are filed as a race in one document and as a monster in another; the setting must choose.

Fishmen — amphibious, changing skin-tone and texture at will, broad-headed and snake-jawed; dangerous in swarms and on muddy shores rather than in open fight. Taken for beasts by nearly everybody. The first man known to have doubted it was Hernan Solana, an Ancoran gentleman, who observed behaviour suggesting at least a mild intelligence.

Giants — as tall as ten men, and nobody knows how. Intelligence ranges from magic-working to simple brutishness. Mostly solitary, and killable by a skilled party; in groups, a matter for armies, and there are towns in the chronicles that are not there any more.

Taer’zal — of the Westerlands, created by a demon lord unknown to the east and now dominant in the south-west of that continent. Quadruped, with an anthropoid torso rising above the forelegs, furred, maned like a lion. They serve as their own mounts and also use chariots.

Beasts, constructs and spirits — bear, boar, cockatrice, dire wolf, lindworm, raven, snake, spider, wolf; automata, golems and illusions; ghosts and elementals bound to a vessel.

What is still wanting

  • Three of the six have no attributes and almost no mechanics. The Children of the Wilds are a faction of one trait apiece.
  • The Hadur have no names of any kind. See Hadur.
  • Four race lists exist and do not agree — the system document’s six, the Lore Overview’s longer taxonomy, the faction list, and this wiki. Reconciling them is open question 3 of the compendium.
  • Gnomes and goblins are one creature filed twice.
  • The Taldar collision — region, race and script-givers on one root — is unresolved.
